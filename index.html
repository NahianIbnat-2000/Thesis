01 / OVERVIEWWhat this thesis asks

Do political credibility signals — measured by the tone and dispersion of climate-related news — shift the greenium, the yield discount on a sovereign green bond relative to a maturity-matched conventional twin? The answer, in India and Indonesia, is no. Three hypotheses, five model specifications, stacked difference-in-differences with HC3 and PCSE standard errors: all null.

These are precise nulls, not noisy ones. Coefficients sit near zero with p-values around 0.4–0.6. The ±6.5 bp confidence interval does contain the Ando et al. benchmark greenium, and a two-country sample limits statistical power — but the VIX result is unambiguous. Global risk appetite dominates daily pricing in thin, illiquid emerging-market green bond segments.

The European benchmark recovers greeniums of −1.82 bp (Germany) and −2.80 bp (Denmark) on the same twin-bond logic, confirming the methodology works — the Asian null reflects market development, not a broken design.

03 / DESIGNEmpirical framework

Each sovereign green bond is paired with a single maturity-matched (10Y) conventional twin from the same issuer. The greenium is the daily yield spread in basis points. Two treatment cohorts are stacked into a single DiD estimator with ±180-day event windows.

Country Event Date Stack
Indonesia JETP accession · G20 Bali Summit 15 Nov 2022 A
India 2nd sovereign green bond auction 27 Oct 2023 B

Identification: F(9,84) = 1.006, p = 0.44 on pre-trend test. Placebo tests at −3, +3, +6 months: all null. Granger causality at lags 2–4 for India (Greenium → Noise) documented in Appendix I. Climate-news tone sourced from GDELT V2Tone via BigQuery.
