How to read this market

Green actions send credibility signals. Navy actions build the receiver — the institutional investors who can actually price your signals.

Below the depth threshold, your greenium is a noisy residual of thin trading: VIX moves it, your announcements don't.

Cross the threshold, and mandate-driven demand anchors a stable greenium — then credibility starts to pay.

Disclaimer for the econometrically inclined: this is a stylized model of one interpretation — the market development threshold argument — from Ibnat, N. (2026), When Governments Say 'Green' — Do Bond Markets Believe Them?, MA Thesis, Central European University. The thesis finds null effects of credibility signals on the greenium in India and Indonesia (with VIX as the dominant pricing driver, 0.35–0.48 bp per SD) and stable greeniums of −1.82/−2.80 bp in Germany/Denmark. The threshold mechanism is consistent with that evidence but is not directly identified in the research design. Replication: github.com/NahianIbnat-2000/Thesis
Briefing · Ministry of Finance

Minister, the bond desk is waiting.

Your country just labelled a sovereign bond green. If investors believe you, they'll accept a lower yield — a negative greenium — cutting your borrowing costs.

You have 20 quarters and 2 action points per quarter. Win by holding the greenium at −2.0 bp or better for 4 consecutive quarters — roughly what Germany earns.

Real-world starting condition: India's mean greenium was +0.5 bp — investors demanded a yield pick-up to hold the green bond. Indonesia's was −6.9 bp, but with ±4.1 bp daily swings: noise, not belief.

One warning: shouting "green" louder may not be the answer.
